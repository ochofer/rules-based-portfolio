# Review of the rules, October 2027

The first review of the rules is on the cycle day (the day each month on which the rules are applied) of October 2027, twelve months after version 1.0, and rule 9 allows no review before it. This agenda was written on 9 October 2026. It changes only by a dated addition at its end, and nothing above an addition is edited.

## The figures the review uses

1. The bootstrap of the allocation test: for each split, the share of ten-year paths whose worst fall is beyond 35 per cent and beyond 40 per cent, in `mandate/simulation_bootstrap.csv`, beside the allocation test in `mandate/allocation_check_results.csv`.
2. The orders of rule 5, placed when the equity weight is outside the band (65 to 75 per cent) on a cycle day, in the record's first twelve months, in `outputs/band_events.csv`, against the mechanics simulation in `mandate/simulation_history.csv`, which placed them on 5 of 323 cycle days on the test's returns.
3. The orders of each month against the allowance of five, in `outputs/orders.csv`, beside the mechanics simulation, which needed at most two orders on a cycle day.
4. The costs of the twelve months: the implementation cost in `outputs/implementation_cost.csv` and its sources by month in `outputs/costs_monthly.csv`, against the cost that the two ETFs' TERs, each at its target weight (70 and 30 per cent), and the half-spreads measured at the orders lead one to expect.
5. The ETF selection run again under the criteria and the ranking of `rules/RULES.md`, with the calendar years 2024 to 2026 in place of 2023 to 2025.
6. The departures register in `outputs/departures.csv`: each departure, its rule, and whether the rule's wording led to it.
7. The tax and residence facts that bear on holding the two ETFs, as they stand at the review.

## The questions it answers

1. Whether the risk limit stands: a worst fall of about one third, tested as 35 per cent, with the outer bound of 40 per cent.
2. Whether the band of 65 to 75 per cent for the equity weight stands.
3. Whether the allowance of five orders a month stands.
4. Whether the two ETFs stand, given the selection run again.
5. Whether a rule's wording needs an erratum or a reading, given the departures.

An answer that changes a rule is a dated amendment under rule 9, and it applies from the first cycle day at least 30 days after its commit.

## Additions

Each addition is dated, and the first is listed first.

- 9 October 2026. Figure 4 compares two different things, so it is read as two. (a) The implementation cost of the twelve months, in `outputs/implementation_cost.csv`, and its sources by month, in `outputs/costs_monthly.csv`, against what the half-spreads measured at the orders, the broker's commission schedule and the currency conversion lead one to expect. (b) The two ETFs' tracking difference against their TERs, which is the cost of owning them.
- 12 October 2026. A sixth question, and the figures it uses. Question 6: whether the portfolio holds a third asset for the case in which equities and government bonds fall together on a rise in inflation, as both of the portfolio's indices did in 2022. Three candidates are tested, on index or price series and not on products: a gold ETC (exchange-traded commodity, a debt security backed by gold held in a vault, since a UCITS fund cannot hold precious metals), a UCITS ETF on a broad commodity index, and a UCITS ETF on euro area government bonds whose payments rise with inflation, which would take its weight from the bond sleeve, the part of the portfolio held in one ETF. Each candidate's series is named before the test runs, with its publisher, its terms and the download date. It is free to download, in month-end values, total return where the asset earns one, converted to euro at the ECB's month-end reference rate, and cached and not redistributed, as the allocation test's sources are. The test is the allocation test's monthly data from February 1999 to December 2025, gross of costs, with the bootstrap of ten-year paths drawn with the same seed (the number that fixes the random choice of blocks) and the same blocks for the portfolio with and without the candidate, so that each path is compared with itself, and with the five historical episodes. The bootstrap table gains the median growth per year (the compound annual return) of the paths. Where a series starts after February 1999, the comparison runs on the months both have, with the 70/30 figures recomputed on those months, and the start is stated. One weight decides, 10 per cent: for the gold ETC and the commodity ETF taken from both sleeves in proportion, equity 63, bonds 27 and the candidate 10 per cent, and for the inflation-linked bonds taken from the bond sleeve, equity 70, nominal bonds 20 and the candidate 10 per cent. The weight of 5 per cent and the other sources of the weight are reported beside it and decide nothing. A candidate answers yes only if, at the deciding weight, the share of paths whose worst fall is beyond 35 per cent is lower by at least one point of the share, and the median growth per year of the paths is not lower, allowing 0.1 point for rounding. A higher median return alone answers no question asked here. Beside the criterion, the review reports for each candidate its fall in each of the five historical episodes beside the portfolio's, the number of months in the sample in which both sleeves fell and the candidate's mean return in those months beside its mean in the others, the criterion on each half of the sample (February 1999 to December 2012, and 2013 to 2025), its ongoing cost, and the count of orders in the mechanics simulation run with three sleeves at the deciding weight. If the sign of the result does not hold in both halves, the answer says that it rests on part of the sample. The sample holds 2022 and not the 1970s, the other inflation episode usually cited, and the answer says so. A yes changes the mandate's constraint that the portfolio holds two ETFs, and for a gold ETC, which is not a UCITS fund, its sentence that both are UCITS funds. It needs rules 1, 4, 5 and 6 written for three sleeves and the product chosen under the selection criteria of rules/RULES.md, and it applies as a dated amendment under rule 9.
