In euro, on the same contributions on the same days, at the issuers' net asset values or the published rate. The comparators carry no costs, and the portfolio's costs are in the Costs part. Returns since the first purchase are cumulative and not annualised. Differences are the comparator's return minus the portfolio's, in percentage points.

The band is the one chance alone produces over the same span: plus or minus twice the tracking error against the comparator, from daily differences, times the square root of the years. The tracking error against the comparator, from daily differences, is the standard deviation of the daily differences between the comparator's return and the portfolio's, times the square root of 252.

A daily comparator's volatility, correlation, difference and band are shown from its 20th daily return, about a month, and its effects with them. The benchmark has values at month ends only, because the bond index has no public daily series. Its return runs from the first month end and is shown from the second, and its volatility, worst fall, correlation, difference and band come from monthly values times the square root of 12 and are shown from the sixth month end, since a tracking error against the benchmark from fewer monthly differences is not an estimate.

Volatility, worst falls and correlations become informative within months, and differences of return over years. Over the record's 31 trading days a difference says nothing about either rule set.

The global market portfolio is a comparator the portfolio's design names, and the benchmark remains the index blend. It holds every asset class at its share of the world's invested market value, as Doeswijk, Lam and Swinkels measure the global multi-asset market portfolio,1 at the weights of 31 December 2023 in their data update, version 6.2 The data update gives two cautions of its own: its private equity is Preqin's figure for private capital, and since the paper countries such as China have entered developed bond indices, so part of emerging debt is counted twice.

It takes the same contributions on the same days. It bought the published weights carried to the day before the first purchase by each class fund's return in euro, holds them as they move with prices, as the market holds them, and invests each top-up in proportion to what it holds. The carried weights are not the market's weights of that day. When the authors publish a new set, the comparator moves to it on the first cycle day after, carried from its own date.

Each class is held through one ETF, the first in the rules' ranking for its class that the account offers, at its net asset value in euro, US dollar values at the ECB's reference rate of the day, without costs. Government bonds and investment-grade credits share one global aggregate bond fund. The account offers that fund only in its distributing class, so the distributing class stands in for the accumulating one and its distributions are added back on their ex-dates. No fund the account offers holds inflation-linked bonds without hedging their currencies, so that class is held in the aggregate fund. Private equity is held through listed private equity companies on developed-market exchanges, the widest index a fund of the class tracks, and they are not the private funds the dataset counts. Real estate is held through listed property companies, which are not the invested real estate the dataset counts. Emerging-market debt is held through local-currency government bonds, 55 per cent of the class in the dataset beside dollar government bonds (18), corporate bonds (16) and inflation-linked bonds (11).

Its equity-like share, equities, private equity and real estate together, is 54.8 per cent in the published weights, 63.9 per cent at the start and 62.5 per cent on 19 November 2026, against the portfolio's 70, and nothing scales the comparator to the portfolio's risk.

The band is the one chance alone produces over the same span: plus or minus twice the tracking error against the comparator, from daily differences, times the square root of the years. The tracking error against the comparator, from daily differences, is the standard deviation of the daily differences between the comparator's return and the portfolio's, times the square root of 252.

Where each difference came fromThe split, the dollar, the regions

Figure 11 | Where each difference came from: from the portfolio's money-weighted return to the comparator's

As of 19 November 2026

Each difference is split into effects computed day by day, summed by month and then over the months. Implementation is reference portfolio A's return minus the portfolio's. Drift is reference portfolio B's return minus reference portfolio A's. Split is the difference in the equity share of the invested part times the equity fund's return in US dollars minus the bond fund's return, and the dollar is the same difference times the gap between the equity fund's return in euro and in US dollars, at the ECB's reference rates, so the two sum to the split in euro. The fund's other currencies stay inside its dollar return. Cash is the difference in cash weight times the return of the invested part.

For a comparator that holds other funds, the difference runs through the two funds held at the comparator's equity share without cash, which gives cash, split and the dollar by the same definitions. Regions is the comparator's equity share times the sum over the four regions of its weight in the region minus the held equity fund's, times the region's MSCI index return in euro, Brinson and Fachler's allocation term, with each fund's weights scaled to the four regions. A US dollar fund's own currency gap against the held fund's goes to the dollar, and within each region is the rest: the funds' choice of companies inside each region, their costs and their timing against the indices. The funds and the indices are valued at different times of the day, so a fund's gap against the indices can reach some tenths of a point on a day and reverse the next. The gap cancels between a comparator and the held fund when both are converted at the ECB's rate, and a gap under a tenth of a point between two rows is within what the valuation times alone produce. The same rules with the first-ranked funds splits its equity fund's return the same way. Region weights are each fund's latest, held for the record so far and kept at each month end. The S&P 500, Nasdaq-100 and Mag7 funds count as North America, a LifeStrategy fund's equity part takes its underlying funds' regions, a FTSE All-World fund taking the held fund's and a FTSE Developed World fund the MSCI World fund's (method/lifestrategy_underlying.csv), and the SPDR funds' come from their holdings by country through method/msci_regions.csv.

For the global market portfolio, the difference runs through the two funds held at its equity-like share, equities, private equity and real estate together, which gives cash, split and the dollar by the same definitions. Equity classes is the weight of private equity and of real estate times each fund's return in euro minus the equity fund's, and bond classes is the weight of each bond class times its fund's return in euro minus the bond fund's. Each dollar fund's conversion to euro goes to the dollar, less the equity fund's for an equity-like class, so the currencies inside a bond fund's dollar return stay in bond classes. Regions and within each region are zero for it.

For savings, the bond fund's return against the rate is split three ways at the ECB curve's spot rate at the bond fund's duration in whole years: the rate against the bonds' yield earned over the days, the duration times the change in that yield, and the rest of the bond return. The LifeStrategy funds hold global bonds hedged to the euro, which the ECB curve does not price, so their bond rows are n/a and their bonds' difference to the bond fund stays within each region.

Timing of contributions is the comparator's money-weighted return minus its time-weighted return, less the portfolio's. Compounding is the difference of the cumulative returns minus the sum of the daily effects. An effect that does not apply to a comparator is zero. Effects are in percentage points to two decimals. What the portfolio does not do, and why

Added on 6 November 2026, after the first cycle day, in answer to the questions readers ask most. The rules are unchanged.

No optimiser. The split is set by the risk limit: 70 per cent equity is the largest target weight whose worst fall on the test's history stayed within 35 per cent (Figure 36). An optimiser would need the expected return of each sleeve, and that is the input the data cannot supply. Over the test's 27 years the standard error of the equity series' mean yearly return is 2.8 percentage points, its yearly volatility over the square root of its years: the uncertainty of that input. The gap in growth per year between the 100/0 and the 40/60 split on the same history is 2.9 points (the table under Figure 36). A circular block bootstrap of the two splits' paired months, which redraws the 27 years 9,999 times in blocks of 12 months, gives the gap a standard error of 2.4 points and a 95 per cent interval from −2.1 to +7.6 points a year, an interval that includes zero, so 27 years do not fix the sign of the gap. DeMiguel, Garlappi and Uppal measured how much history a sample-based optimiser needs before it beats an equal split out of sample: about 3,000 months for 25 assets and about 6,000 for 50.4 A replication of their study by the author of this page finds that none of the seven optimising rules it tests earns a higher Sharpe ratio than the equal split at the 5 per cent level, on any of four sets of assets, over the 261 months since the paper.5 The frontier of the two sleeves is in the Risk view (Figure 31). With two assets it is a line, and the limit picks the point on it.

The benchmark, and why not the S&P 500. A benchmark is the portfolio a client would hold without the manager, so that the manager's contribution can be measured as the difference and bounded. This portfolio's benchmark is the blend of its two funds' own indices at the target weights, rebalanced at each month end to the target weights in force on that day, the policy the rules implement. The difference to it is the cost of implementing that policy: the funds' costs and tracking differences, the band's drift from the target weights, cash waiting for the cycle day, and trading. The rules take no position against the benchmark, so there is no active return to claim, and the difference is expected to be small and negative. The two reference portfolios split it into its parts: reference A, the same contributions under the same rules at net asset value, and reference B, the same contributions returned to the target weights on every cycle day (Figure 3). No single market index is the benchmark. The question readers ask most is why the portfolio does not simply hold the S&P 500. The global equity fund already holds the index's companies at about 64 per cent of its weight, so holding the index alone sells the rest of the world's listed companies and puts the whole portfolio into one country and one currency. A weight on one country above its share of the world is a view, and the rules take none. The advice to hold the index alone grew popular in the years after 2009, when the US stock market returned 597.7 per cent in dollars, dividends included, against 133.0 per cent for the developed markets outside the United States, and over the ten years from 2000 to 2009 the same two series returned minus 3.7 per cent and 29.6 per cent.6 On the limit, Figure 39 repeats the allocation test with the US stock market in euro in place of the global series, with the same splits, the same band rule and the same months: the largest split within 35 per cent is 65/35, and the worst fall at 70/30 is 36.0 per cent, from August 2000 to February 2003. The two lines are two rule sets over the record's months, a span on which the gap says nothing about either.

No views. A view is a statement that an asset's expected return differs from what its price implies, held with a stated confidence. The standard method for turning views into weights starts from the market portfolio and tilts it by each view in proportion to the confidence in it.7 With no views the method returns the market portfolio, and that is what the equity sleeve holds, at the weights of the world's listed companies. The one judgement in the rules is the one behind holding equity at all, that its return exceeds the return on bonds over five to ten years, and the limit sets how much of the portfolio rides on it.

No signal. A signal is a view produced by a rule applied to data and tested on history. None enters the portfolio. The value of a forecasting skill grows with the square root of the number of independent bets it is applied to,8 and a portfolio of two funds with one decision a year makes about one bet a year, so no skill would be detectable in its record and none would be worth its cost. At this portfolio's size the orders a signal calls for cost more than the signal could earn, and the cost of each order is shown under Costs in the Performance view. A portfolio that carries a signal also becomes a test of that signal, and a few years of one portfolio cannot run that test. Signals belong in a separate study on decades of data, and a signal that passes its test there can enter the portfolio only through the yearly review of the rules, as a dated amendment with its test fixed in advance.9 The first review is in October 2027 and its agenda is public.

Split is the difference in equity weight at the previous month end times the global equity series' return in US dollars minus the bonds' return, and the dollar is the same difference times the gap between that series' return in euro and in US dollars. For the US stock market at 70/30, the US stock market in dollars is its equity weight at the previous month end times the US market's return in dollars less the global series'. Both equity series are converted at the same rate, so that comparator's dollar row holds the drift term and the conversion's cross term only, and the currencies inside the global series' dollar return stay in that return, as on the record. The split of the US stock market at 70/30 is nonzero because the two paths' equity weights drift apart between rebalancings, and the band triggers on different month ends when the two equity series differ.

Calendar years are cumulative, in points, and 1999 runs from February. The whole test and the two periods are per year, in points a year: the difference of the two growth rates per year, with each effect as its monthly sum divided by the span's years. Compounding is the difference less the sum of the effects. It includes the volatility drag of the more volatile path, which is why it can carry either sign.

The band chance alone produces is twice the tracking error against the comparator, from monthly differences, times the square root of the years for a calendar year, and divided by it for a longer span. Each comparator's tracking error, from monthly differences, is the standard deviation of its monthly return less 70/30's over the whole test, times the square root of 12: 4.52 points a year for 100/0, 1.65 points a year for 60/40 and 2.40 points a year for the US stock market at 70/30. Volatility and the worst fall are the comparator's own within each span.

The rules

Rendered from rules/RULES.md at build time, with the amendments table

These are the rules the portfolio runs under. Version 1.0 was fixed on 7 October 2026, before the first order, and the git tag rules-v1.0 marks it. Version 1.1 adds rule 11 by the amendment of 20 November 2026, and the git tag rules-v1.1 marks it. Version 1.0 applies until 5 January 2027, and version 1.1 from that day. A rule changes only through rule 9.

The portfolio is long-only and holds two exchange-traded funds in a euro brokerage account. An ETF (exchange-traded fund) is a fund whose shares are listed on a stock exchange. Both ETFs held here are UCITS funds, the European Union standard for funds sold to the public. One ETF holds global equities and the other holds euro government bonds.

Terms used in the rules

  • Sleeve: the part of the portfolio held in one ETF. The portfolio has an equity sleeve and a bond sleeve.
  • Weight: a sleeve's value divided by the value of the portfolio. The value of the portfolio is the value of both ETFs plus any contribution not yet invested.
  • Target weight: 70 per cent for the equity sleeve and 30 per cent for the bond sleeve.
  • Band: the interval from 65 to 75 per cent for the equity weight.
  • Worst fall: the largest fall in the portfolio's value from a previous peak to a later low, measured on returns with contributions removed.
  • Contribution: money paid into the portfolio, meaning the starting amount and each top-up.
  • Top-up: the fixed monthly contribution.
  • Cycle day: the day each month on which the rules are applied.
  • Trading day: a day on which Xetra, the Frankfurt exchange where both ETFs are listed in euro, is open.
  • Order: an instruction to the broker to buy or sell one ETF.
  • Ledger: the private record of every contribution and every order.

Objective and risk

The mandate in mandate/MANDATE.md sets the objective and the risk limit. The objective is the highest long-run growth within a worst fall of about one third, over a horizon of five to ten years with no withdrawals, and a worst fall above 40 per cent is outside it. The target weights follow from the limit: 70 per cent is the largest equity target weight whose worst fall, in euro from February 1999 to December 2025 and rebalanced by a band as in rule 5, stayed within 35 per cent (34.7 per cent, from August 2000 to March 2003). The test and its data sources are in mandate/allocation_check.py, and its output is in mandate/allocation_check_results.md. The limit is the basis of the target weights and never a reason to sell. In a fall, rule 8 applies and the cycle runs as written.

The rules

  1. Target weights. The portfolio holds two sleeves, each in one ETF, at target weights of 70 per cent equity and 30 per cent bonds. There is no cash target. Cash is whatever part of a contribution has not yet been invested.

  2. Contributions. A starting amount is fixed before the first order. Every month after that, a top-up equal to 5 per cent of the starting amount is paid into the account by the 2nd. The euro amounts are private, and the fingerprint in the last section fixes them.

  3. The cycle. The cycle day is the 5th of each month, or the first trading day after the 5th when the 5th is not a trading day. On the cycle day the weights are computed from the ledger and the orders the rules produce are placed the same day.

  4. Top-ups rebalance first. The top-up buys the sleeve furthest below its target weight, up to the amount that brings that sleeve to its target weight. Any remainder buys both sleeves in proportion to their target weights. Nothing is sold while the equity weight is inside the band. With two sleeves, this places the portfolio at its target weights whenever the top-up is large enough, and otherwise moves it as close to them as buying alone allows. For example, with the starting amount set to 100, equity worth 68 and bonds worth 32 before a top-up of 5, the portfolio is worth 105 after the top-up and the equity target is 73.5. Equity is 5.5 short, more than the top-up, so all 5 buy equity and the equity weight moves from 68.0 to 69.5 per cent.

  5. The band. If the equity weight is below 65 or above 75 per cent after the top-up, the portfolio is brought back to exactly 70 and 30 per cent on the same day, by selling the sleeve above its target weight and buying the other. Inside the band nothing is sold. There is no calendar rebalancing. The band is the only trigger.

  6. Order budget. At most five orders are placed in a month, the number the account's plan carries without commission. If the rules call for more, the band orders come first and the top-up waits for the next cycle day. Rules 4 and 5 never need more than two orders on a cycle day.

  7. Execution. Orders are placed on a trading day between 15:45 and 17:00 Amsterdam time, when Xetra and the US equity market are both open, outside the first fifteen minutes of the US session and the last thirty minutes of the Xetra session. For each order the ledger records the date, the time, the ISIN, the side, the units, the price, the fee, the currency, and the bid and ask quoted when the order was placed.

  8. No discretion. No order is placed outside these rules. News does not add a purchase, a fall in prices does not pause the cycle, and no ETF is changed. When in doubt, the cycle is run as written and the doubt is recorded in the monthly log.

  9. Changing the rules. A rule changes only by a dated amendment committed to this file. An amendment applies from the first cycle day at least 30 days after its commit. The first review of the rules is on the cycle day of October 2027, twelve months after this version, and there is no review before it.

  10. Sales. Every sale is recorded in the ledger on its day with the units sold, the proceeds, the cost basis and the realised gain or loss. The cost basis is the average price paid per unit, fees included, times the units sold.

  11. The target follows the allocation test. Added by the amendment of 20 November 2026, this rule applies from the cycle day of 5 January 2027. Each month end from 30 November 2026 gives a reading of the allocation test of mandate/allocation_check.py, or gives none. A reading is the test run on the equity ETF's weights by region in the issuer's holdings file dated the month's last trading day on Xetra's calendar, or, where the issuer publishes none for that day, in the first file dated after it and before the cycle day that follows the month end. The rules count as applied on a cycle day once its cycle report or its row of outputs/allocation_targets.csv is written. A month end gives no reading when it has no such file or no row written in time, when the test runs on files of Kenneth French's library other than the five whose hashes are committed in mandate/allocation_check_files.csv, or on values of the European Central Bank's three series other than those whose digests are committed there, or when the holdings file has a country that method/french_regions.csv does not place. The record says so in a row for that month end, with the reason, and later month ends are still read. A row written after that is read on no cycle day. French's library serves only its latest files, so a lost file can be downloaded again only while the library serves the same database. Once it does not, no month end gives a reading until an amendment names new files. The European Central Bank serves each of its series whole, so a lost file can be downloaded again. If a value the test reads has been revised by then, no month end gives a reading until an amendment names the new values. Where no split, a pair of target weights on the test's grid, has a worst fall within 35 per cent, the reading is none. On each cycle day the two month ends before it are read, and both must give a reading: a month end without one breaks the pair, so the two cycle days that read it change nothing. When both give a reading of the same split, other than none, and it differs from the target weights, that split becomes the target weights from that cycle day, in either direction, the band becomes the interval 5 points either side of the new equity target weight, and rules 1, 4 and 5 are read with them. In every other case the target weights do not change. The change itself adds no order and places no sale. The top-ups of rule 4 move the equity weight towards the new target, and rule 5 applies with the new band from the first cycle day on which the equity weight is inside it. Until then the edge, on that side, of the last band the equity weight was inside stays in force, and beyond it rule 5 brings the portfolio to the new target weights. The test's limit, its history, its sources and its settings change only by an amendment, and a country is added to method/french_regions.csv only by an amendment.

How the two ETFs were chosen

Criteria. An equity ETF qualifies if it tracks a global index of developed and emerging markets (FTSE All-World, MSCI ACWI, MSCI ACWI IMI, or an equivalent global index tracked by a Vanguard, iShares or SPDR fund), is a UCITS fund, accumulates, is listed in euro on a European exchange, replicates physically, charges a TER of at most 0.25%, has a fund size above EUR 1 billion and is offered in euro on the account. A bond ETF qualifies if it tracks a euro aggregate or euro government index of investment-grade bonds, either across all maturities or capped at 10 years, is a UCITS fund, accumulates, holds bonds denominated in euro, charges a TER of at most 0.20%, has a fund size above EUR 500 million and is offered in euro on the account. Versions of an index that change its exposure fail: ESG and other screened indices, indices built on a factor (a company characteristic, such as dividend yield, by which an index selects or weights companies) and currency-hedged share classes. An index of short maturities only or long maturities only fails. Synthetic replication, in which the fund holds other securities and receives the index return from a bank through a swap contract, fails.

Ranking. Among the ETFs that qualify, the order was fixed before the funds were compared. Equity ETFs rank first by Irish domicile, then by the highest average tracking difference over 2023, 2024 and 2025, then by the lowest TER. Bond ETFs rank by the highest average tracking difference over the same years, then by the lowest TER. Tracking difference is an ETF's calendar-year return minus the return of its index, both taken from the issuer's factsheet, so a higher figure means a smaller shortfall. An ETF with fewer than three full calendar years of returns ranks after the others, by TER. Irish domicile comes first for equity because a fund domiciled in Ireland pays 15 per cent tax on US dividends under the treaty between the two countries, where a fund domiciled in Luxembourg pays 30 per cent. With the United States at about 60 per cent of a global index and a dividend yield near 1.3 per cent, the difference is about 0.12 percentage points a year.

Terms

The terms the dashboard uses, each with its definition.

Active return
the portfolio's return less the benchmark's.
Active weight
a component's weight in the portfolio less its weight in the benchmark.
Allocation test
the historical test in mandate/allocation_check.py that derived the split from the risk limit, on monthly euro returns from February 1999 to December 2025. From 5 January 2027 rule 11 reads it again at each month end.
Band
the interval from 65 to 75 per cent for the equity weight. When the equity weight is outside it on a cycle day, after the top-up, the portfolio is brought back to its target weights.
Band chance alone produces
plus or minus twice the tracking error against a comparator, times the square root of the years, around zero. It is the difference between two series that chance alone produces over the same span. It ignores compounding and treats the daily differences as independent.
Basis point
one hundredth of a percentage point.
Bond classes
for the global market portfolio, the weight of each bond class times its fund's return in euro minus the held bond ETF's, less each fund's conversion from US dollars, which goes to the dollar.
Bootstrap
the resampling of the allocation test's returns in blocks of twelve consecutive months, each starting at a random month, into paths of five and ten years, with the worst fall measured on each path.
Cap-weighted
holding each security in proportion to its market value, as the indices of both ETFs do.
Comparator
a portfolio that receives the same contributions on the same days as the portfolio, valued in euro without costs and set beside the record in the Comparisons part, e.g., a reference portfolio, another split under the same rules, the global market portfolio, or savings at the euro short-term rate.
Component
one of the four equity regions and three maturity groups into which the look-through divides the portfolio to estimate its risk, with cash, which has no return.
Contribution
money paid into the portfolio, i.e., the starting amount and each top-up, which is 5 per cent of the starting amount.
Covariance
how far two returns move together, in the units of variance, the square of volatility: their correlation times the product of their standard deviations.
Cycle day
the day each month on which the rules are applied. It is the 5th, or the first trading day after it when the 5th is not one.
Cycle report
the file cycles/YYYY-MM.md, written on a cycle day before any order, with each rule that bears on the proposed orders, its figure and a pass or a fail, and one line after the orders that compares them with the proposal. The report is committed and pushed before the first order of the day, and the line after the orders is a second commit.
Deciding series
the allocation test's equity series, Kenneth French's market returns for five regions at the equity ETF's weights by region, the same weights in every month of the history. The two MSCI series beside it choose nothing.
Departure
an order placed, or left unplaced, other than as the rules require. The departures register lists each one with its rule and its consequence.
Drawdown
the fall in the portfolio's value below its previous peak on a given day, with contributions removed, in per cent.
Drift effect
reference portfolio A's return minus reference portfolio B's. It is the effect of letting the weights drift inside the band, before costs.
Duration
a bond's average time to its payments, weighted by their value, in years. It is also, approximately, the percentage fall in the bond's price when its yield rises by one percentage point. For the bond sleeve it is the average duration in the issuer's factsheet.
Efficient frontier
the long-only, fully invested portfolios of the components with the lowest estimated volatility for each estimated return, on the means and covariances of one window of 120 months.
Equity classes
for the global market portfolio, the weight of private equity and of real estate times each class fund's return in euro minus the held equity ETF's, less each fund's dollar conversion beyond the equity ETF's, which goes to the dollar.
Estimated tracking error against the benchmark
the standard deviation of the active return over a year that the components' covariance implies for today's active weights. Jorion (2003) calls it tracking-error volatility.
Estimated volatility
the standard deviation of the portfolio's return over a year, estimated from the components' monthly returns over the last 120 months at the latest look-through weights.
Euro short-term rate
the rate at which banks borrow euro overnight without collateral, which the European Central Bank publishes each working day.
Expected shortfall
the mean loss at or beyond the value at risk. The historical figure is the mean of the six worst months of the 120-month window, and the normal figure is the normal distribution's mean beyond the value at risk.
Factor
one of the return series in Kenneth French's data library for developed markets: the market's return minus the risk-free rate, and the returns of long-short portfolios for size, value, profitability, investment and momentum.
Forecast's calibration
the standard deviation, over the months that have a forecast, of each month's return divided by the monthly volatility estimated for it at the end of the month before. 1 means the estimates matched the returns, and above 1 that they were too low.
Global market portfolio
every asset class held at its share of the world's invested market value, here by the weights of Doeswijk, Lam and Swinkels (2014, data update 2024) at the end of 2023, held through one ETF per class.
Growth per year
the compound annual return, with contributions removed.
Half-spread
half the gap between the ask and the bid quoted when an order is placed.
Implementation cost
reference portfolio A's value relative to the portfolio's, with contributions removed, in basis points, positive when the portfolio's value is below reference portfolio A's. It is negative when the portfolio's value is above.
In-sample
computed on the data on which a choice was made, here the returns from 1999 to 2025 on which the split was chosen.
Index blend
MSCI ACWI net total return and the Bloomberg Euro-Aggregate Treasury index, in euro, at the target weights, rebalanced at each month end to the target weights in force on that day, the portfolio's benchmark. The target weights are 70 and 30 per cent until rule 11 changes them, and a change enters the blend at the first month end after the cycle day it applies from.
Ledger
the private record of every contribution and every order. It is kept outside the repository.
Loading
the coefficient of the ETF's return minus the risk-free rate on a factor's return.
Look-through
the portfolio's exposure to the securities inside the two ETFs, each ETF's holdings weighted by its weight in the portfolio.
Market effect
a month's change in the portfolio's value that did not come from contributions, in per cent of the value at the start of the month.
Maturity group
the bonds of the bond sleeve with 1 to 5, 5 to 10 or over 10 years to maturity, each priced as a zero-coupon euro area government bond at 3, 7.5 or 20 years from the European Central Bank's yield curve.
Mechanics simulation
the rules applied to the allocation test's returns, with a starting amount of 100 and a top-up of 5 a month, for the counts of their orders.
Minimum-variance portfolio
the portfolio on the efficient frontier with the lowest estimated volatility.
Money-weighted return
the rate at which the contributions, each from its date, grow to the portfolio's value. The timing of the contributions therefore counts. It is cumulative in the first year and per year after it.
Monthly note
the note of 120 to 200 words, in notes/YYYY-MM.md, written by hand after each cycle day.
Net asset value
the value of one share of an ETF at the close of a day, as its issuer publishes it.
Ongoing costs
what an ETF takes from its assets each year, as its key information document states them, inside its net asset value. They are its TER plus its transaction costs.
Portfolio
the two ETFs held under these rules, plus any contribution not yet invested.
Produced by the rules
a column of outputs/orders.csv. It reads yes for an order the rules produce on its cycle day, no for an order they do not produce, and starting amount for the two first purchases, which invested the starting amount at the target weights on 8 October 2026, a day no rule names.
Realised tracking error against the benchmark
the standard deviation of the monthly active returns, times the square root of 12.
Realised volatility
the standard deviation of the portfolio's last 12 monthly time-weighted returns, times the square root of 12, so a figure for a year.
Rebalancing
moving the weights back towards the target weights, through top-ups (rule 4) or through the band (rule 5).
Record
what the program computes from the ledger and the prices. It covers holdings, weights, orders and their costs, returns, the look-through and the reference portfolios.
Reference portfolio
a portfolio computed from the same contributions and prices as the portfolio, without costs. Reference portfolio A follows the same rules, and reference portfolio B goes back to the target weights on every cycle day.
Regions
the effect of holding the four MSCI regions in other weights than the held equity ETF does: the comparator's equity share times the sum over the regions of its weight minus the held ETF's, times the region's index return in euro, the allocation term of Brinson and Fachler (1985).
Resampled paths
the bootstrap's ten-year paths carried forward from the first purchase with the top-up and the rules, in units of the starting amount.
Risk contribution
a component's weight times the covariance of its return with the portfolio's return, divided by the portfolio's variance. The contributions sum to 100 per cent.
Risk limit
the worst fall the mandate aims to stay within, about one third, tested as 35 per cent.
Risk-free rate
the one-month US Treasury bill return in French's files.
Rolling correlation
the correlation of two series of monthly returns over the 36 months to each month end.
S&P 500 alternative
the same contributions on the same days into one S&P 500 ETF at its net asset value in euro, in fractional units and without costs, starting with the starting amount at the net asset value of the day of the first purchase, as reference portfolio A starts. It stands for the rule "hold the S&P 500 and nothing else" with no costs, so that it is compared with reference portfolio A like for like, two rule sets on the same money.
Sharpe ratio
the mean of a portfolio's return above the risk-free rate, divided by the standard deviation of that excess return, so the return earned per unit of volatility.
Sleeve
the part of the portfolio held in one ETF.
Split
a pair of target weights, written equity/bonds, e.g., 60/40.
Standard error
the estimated standard deviation of an estimate.
Starting amount
the first contribution, fixed before the first order.
Target weight
the weight the rules aim for in a sleeve. Rule 1 sets 70 and 30 per cent for equity and bonds, rule 11 can change them, and the dashboard gives those in force.
TER
total expense ratio, the management and other operating costs an ETF takes from its assets each year, as a percentage of them.
Time-weighted return
the return with contributions removed, chained from day to day. The timing and the size of the contributions therefore do not affect it.
Top-up
the fixed monthly contribution, 5 per cent of the starting amount.
Tracking difference
an ETF's return minus its index's return over a calendar year, in percentage points.
Tracking error
the standard deviation of the difference between the portfolio's return and a named reference's, as a figure for a year. The tracking error against a comparator is taken from daily differences, times the square root of 252.
Trading day
a day on which Xetra, the Frankfurt exchange where both ETFs are listed in euro, is open.
Value at risk
the loss over one month at 95 per cent at today's weights. It is given historical or normal. The historical figure is the sixth worst month of the 120-month window, a loss reached or exceeded in six months of the 120, and the normal figure comes from the normal distribution with the estimated volatility.
Weight
a sleeve's value divided by the portfolio's.
Within each region
what a comparator's difference to the two ETFs held at its equity share leaves after regions and the dollar: the choice of companies inside each region, the funds' costs and their timing against the indices.
Worst fall
the largest fall in value from a previous peak to a later low, with contributions removed.
